### S2E06 — THE SEA OF BILASPUR

**Purpose:** November 24, 1933 — Bilaspur. Gandhi promised this city in 1920. The coins thrown that hurt Gandhi. Srivastava's foot crushed in the crowd. The women's meeting and the jewelry donation. The locked hostel — and student Ramsharan Tamboli running to the station.

**Source Chapter:** 1933: Second Visit — Gandhi in Bilaspur, November 24

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: Gandhi in an open car moving through Bilaspur. Coins raining down from the crowd. Volunteers surrounding the car, some being lifted off their feet by the press of people. Gandhi raising a hand — half greeting, half shield.
Text: *"People were showering coins, which was also hurting Gandhi."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: 1920. Gandhi at Raipur. The people of Bilaspur beg him to come. He promises: *soon.*
- Slide 3: Thirteen years. *"The people's craze for Gandhi had not diminished."* Weeks before his arrival, people camp in Bilaspur to secure a place.
- Slide 4: Barristers Chhedilal, Kunjbihari Agnihotri, Dr. Shivdulare Mishra, Amar Singh Sehgal — organising.
- Slide 5: November 24. Eight in the morning. Gandhi reaches Bilaspur. Dawn crowds. No place to walk on the main road.
- Slide 6: His open car moves like an ant through the mass. Coins rain from buildings. Some coins hit Gandhi.
- Slide 7: Volunteer Yadunandan Prasad Srivastava, clearing the path in front of the car, is gripped by the crowd — *"his feet got uprooted from the ground and he got hit by the road."*

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
Gandhi rests at Kunjbehari Agnihotri's residence — greeted from the window by the uncontrollable crowd outside. Company Garden (now Vivekananda Udyan) — the women's meeting. Women present a bag of Rs 1000. Gandhi considers it little as aid to independence. The women immediately give their jewelry. Walking to the car after the women's meeting, one of Gandhi's feet on the car, one on the ground — someone grabs his leg. Srivastava, without hesitation, presses the person's hand with his foot. He writes: *"The work was of cruelty, but Gandhi lost his foot."* The public meeting. The Shanichari Padav meeting. The locked hostel — warden locks students in to prevent them from seeing Gandhi. Student Ramsharan Tamboli spots Gandhi's convoy through the hockey field. Runs to the station. Gandhi at the door of the train, bag spread. Signals Tamboli through the crowd. Tamboli, gasping, offers a Duanni (two-paise coin). Gandhi accepts.

**Key Moment:**
*"The work was of cruelty, but Gandhi lost his foot"* — Srivastava writing about crushing the hand of the person who grabbed Gandhi's leg, preventing him from falling.

**Key Moment 2:**
Ramsharan Tamboli — a hostel student locked in against his will — running to the station and making his offering. The coin was almost nothing. The moment was everything.

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
Gandhi boards the evening train back to Raipur. A student stands on the platform gasping, having just offered a two-paise coin. Gandhi has already moved on. The student will remember this for the rest of his life.

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
Bilaspur waited thirteen years. The day lasted a few hours. It was enough.

**Cliffhanger (Slide 37):**
*"The roads of Chhattisgarh. The villages that have no names in history books. Episode 7: I Am a Baniya."*

### S2E07 — I AM A BANIYA

**Purpose:** The intimate human moments of the 1933 visit — the Naapi story, the turmeric bundle, the old woman at Nandghat, the Dhamtari visit on November 25. Gandhi's wit, warmth, and common humanity. The episode that reveals the man inside the Mahatma.

**Source Chapter:** 1933: Second Visit — Dhamtari November 25, various human moments

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: Gandhi on a dusty road near Nandghat. A 70-year-old Harijan woman standing in the road, blocking his car, a flower garland in her hands. Gandhi looking at her from the car window. Gentle eyes.
Text: *"I am a Harijan woman, I want to wash Gandhi's feet once before dying and offer them flowers."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: November 24 — near Nandghat. An old woman stands in the road. Gandhi's car stops.
- Slide 3: *"I am a Harijan woman, I want to wash Gandhi's feet once before dying and offer them flowers."*
- Slide 4: Gandhi (with a laugh): *"I'll take one rupee for that."* She cannot afford one rupee in 1933. She is frustrated.
- Slide 5: She says: *"You stay here, Baba, I am in search of my house."* Gandhi thinks it jest. She goes. Then Gandhi puts one foot forward.
- Slide 6: Her wish is complete. The car moves on. She stands in the road alone, holding the moment.
- Slide 7: November 25. Dhamtari. Gandhi arrives at ten-thirty in the morning.

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
Dhamtari November 25. Gandhi presented with an honorarium by the Municipal Corporation — he auctions it immediately at the meeting, bid of Rs 15. A bundle of letters arrives — Mahadev Desai replies to them all. Gandhi at Chhotelal Srivastava's home. Women's meeting outside. The Naapi story — Meeraben telling Dr. Hazarilal Jain to bring *"the Naapi."* Confusion. A hairdresser named Makhan brought from Brahmanpara. Gandhi shaved. Afterwards Gandhi to Hazarilal: *"Give a rupee."* Makhan falls at Gandhi's feet: *"To whom millions yearn to see, I touched him myself — this is my reward."* Gandhi: *"Feed a green grass of one and a quarter rupees to a cow."* At Cornaband Chowk in Raipur — a bundle falls into Gandhi's motor from the crowd. Opened: a turmeric lump, yellow rice, two cauldrons, a half-coin, some flowers. Gandhi: *"It is a gift of a true poor."* The bundle auctioned at Nawapara-Rajim for Rs 101.

**Key Dialogue:**
Makhan the barber (Chhattisgarhi): *"Jekar Darasan Bar Laakhon Logan Taras Rahithen, Taun La Main Aaj Chhoo Lenv, Ihee Mor Nyochhaavar He"* — *"To whom millions of people yearn to see, I touched him myself, this is my reward."*

Gandhi: *"Feed a green grass of one and a quarter rupees to a cow."*

Gandhi (on the turmeric bundle): *"It is a gift of a true poor."*

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
The turmeric bundle auctioned for Rs 101. A wordless gift from an unknown hand — valued by Gandhi above all the gold rings and honoraria.

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
November 26. The rural visits begin — Saragaon, Kharora, Palari, Kanaki, Balodabazar, Simga. Gandhi moves through villages that have no history books, only living memory.

**Cliffhanger (Slide 37):**
*"The village path has no end. Episode 8: The Village Path."*

### S2E08 — THE VILLAGE PATH

**Purpose:** November 26–27, 1933 — the rural visits. Saragaon, Kharora, Palari, Kanaki, Balodabazar, Simga on the 26th. Dumartarai, Mana, Abhanpur, Bhotidih, Maraud, Kurud on the 27th. The grassroots reality of Gandhi's mission. Chhattisgarh at its most ordinary — and most extraordinary.

**Source Chapters:** 1933: Second Visit — November 26–27; Chhattisgarh at the Grassroots; Gandhian Heritages in Chhattisgarh

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: Gandhi walking barefoot on a dusty village road in Chhattisgarh. Small mud-hut village behind him, a gathering of villagers ahead. Children running toward him. Late afternoon golden light.
Text: *"Wherever Gandhi went, thousands of people gathered to see him, people were eager to see him, hear him and touch his feet."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: November 26. The rural circuit begins. Six villages in one day.
- Slide 3: People arriving from far and wide — on foot, by Bailgadi (bull cart) — a week before Gandhi's scheduled arrival.
- Slide 4: Flowers and coins showered on Gandhi at every stop on the route.
- Slide 5: At places not on the official schedule, people stand on roadsides just to see the car pass.
- Slide 6: November 27. Six more villages. Dhamtari route: Dumartarai, Mana, Abhanpur, Bhotidih, Maraud, Kurud.
- Slide 7: Each village — a small meeting. The message of untouchability. The message of Harijan dignity.

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
Gandhi visits Kajuram Verma in Saragaon — a freedom fighter who travelled to Delhi campaigning against British rule. The intelligence department soldier assigned to follow him, who becomes moved by Kejuram's speech and eventually resigns from government service to join the Satyagraha. The story of the Bhai Dooj — Kejuram missing his sister's festival because he must march on. The soldier going to Kejuram's village to deliver his message. The sister feeding the soldier as she would have fed her brother. Giving him a Khadi dhoti meant for Kejuram. The soldier moved to tears — *"I too will join the personal Satyagraha and go to jail."* He does. He later becomes an MLA in Uttar Pradesh. In the evenings, Gandhi returns to Raipur. The night journeys by train. The primary responsibility of arrangement resting on Pt. Ravi Shankar Shukla — but since no single person accompanies Gandhi through the entire journey, details are gathered from many sources.

**Key Moment — The Intelligence Soldier:**
*"I can no longer be a policeman. I too will join the personal Satyagraha and go to jail."* He resigned from government service on the spot, moved by Khadi cloth and a sister's love.

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
The evening of November 27. Gandhi has been through twelve villages in two days. Untouchability spoken against at each stop. The message carried into soil and memory. Tomorrow: the last day.

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
November 28. Early morning. Gandhi prepares to leave for Balaghat by train. From there, to Katni. From there — away from Chhattisgarh, forever.

**Cliffhanger (Slide 37):**
*"He will not come back. But something of him stays. Episode 9: Johar Gandhi — The Farewell."*

### S2E09 — JOHAR GANDHI

**Purpose:** November 28, 1933 — the departure. Gandhi boards the train for Balaghat. The reckoning with what his two visits achieved and what they could not. The word *Johar* — the Chhattisgarhi salute — as the episode's emotional core. The independence of 1947 as epilogue.

**Source Chapters:** 1933: Second Visit — November 28; Modern Chhattisgarh; Gandhian Heritages; Gandhian Pioneers

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: Raipur railway station, November 28, 1933. Dawn. Gandhi in his dhoti and shawl, bag in hand, walking toward the train. A crowd watches. No jostling. Just watching. And the word floating above in Chhattisgarhi script: *JOHAR.*
Text: *"Gandhi went to Itarsi via Balaghat. Gandhi came to Chhattisgarh twice — the first time as Mohandas, the second time as a Mahatma."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: November 28. Eight in the morning. Gandhi leaves for Balaghat by train.
- Slide 3: He will never return to Chhattisgarh.
- Slide 4: *Johar* — the Chhattisgarhi word of greeting and farewell. An expression of respect, of seeing, of meeting. Johar Gandhi.
- Slide 5: The train pulls away. Faces on the platform. Some weeping. Most simply watching.
- Slide 6: What did he leave? A Congress Committee. A Harijan fund. A word against untouchability in every village. A spinning wheel in every home.
- Slide 7: August 15, 1947. India is free.

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
What freedom looked like in Chhattisgarh. Pt. Ravi Shankar Shukla — the first Chief Minister of Madhya Pradesh and Berar — hoisting the tricolor at Sitabardi Fort in Nagpur. RK Patil in Raipur. Ghanshyam Singh Gupta in Durg. Ram Gopal Tiwari in Bilaspur. Gandhi shot dead in Delhi, January 1948. Einstein's words: *"Future generations will find it difficult to believe that such a man made of flesh and blood ever came to earth."* The leaders of Chhattisgarh who gave their lives: a long roll call. Veer Narayan Singh at Jaistambh Chowk. Gaind Singh before the palace of Paralkot. The 17 soldiers of Raipur. Gundadhur — still missing. Dayavati — a farmer who stood before a gun. Makhan the barber — who asked only to touch Gandhi once. Abbas Bhai — whose license was cancelled for driving Gandhi to Dhamtari.

**Key Dialogue:**
Gandhi (January 30, 1948 — final words recorded in the book's spirit): His assassination. Einstein's tribute. The silence that followed.

*"Today Gandhi is remembered only on 2nd October. Today, keeping Gandhi's ideology at bay, efforts are being made to serve all kinds of interests with the help of violence."* — Amir Hashmi

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
The founding of Chhattisgarh as a separate state — November 1, 2000. A land that fought for 175 years finally named on its own. *Johar.*

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
The question Amir Hashmi asks in the Introduction, answered: *"If one questions the relevance of Gandhi and his ideology even in the presence of violence, extremism, poverty, and inequality, then perhaps there is some ambiguity in that person's understanding of Gandhi's ideology."*

**Cliffhanger (Slide 37):**
*"The pioneers. The names that remain. Episode 10: The Gandhian Pioneers."*

### S2E10 — THE GANDHIAN PIONEERS

**Purpose:** The finale. A tribute episode. The Gandhian Pioneers of Chhattisgarh — each major freedom fighter given their full portrait. The Gandhian Heritages — the places that carry Gandhi's memory today. The series closes with the living legacy and a final *Johar.*

**Source Chapters:** Gandhian Pioneers of Chhattisgarh; Gandhian Heritages in Chhattisgarh; Modern Chhattisgarh

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: A gallery of portraits — every major figure from both series — arranged like a constellation against the night sky of Chhattisgarh. Stars, each one a name.
Text: *"Thousands of leaders were influenced by Gandhi's personality and engaged in the service of the country for life."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: Gandhi Chowk, Raipur — the ground where Gandhi spoke in 1920. Still there.
- Slide 3: The Jaitusav Math — where Harijans entered the temple doors in 1933. Still there.
- Slide 4: Moti Bagh — where one lakh people gathered. Still there.
- Slide 5: Kandel village — where the Satyagraha began. Still there.
- Slide 6: Gandhigram Tamora — where Dayavati stood. Still there. Named for him.
- Slide 7: The places hold what the people put into them.

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
Portraits and arcs of the major Gandhian Pioneers — Pt. Ravi Shankar Shukla, Pt. Sundarlal Sharma, Babu Chhotelal Srivastava, Thakur Pyarelal Singh, Dr. Khubchand Baghel, Ghanshyam Singh Gupta, E. Raghavendra Rao, Maulana Abdul Rauf Khan. The complete Satyagrahi index from the book. The women pioneers — Dr. Radhabai, Dayavati, the unnamed women of Tanwat-Nawapara and Tamora and Bilaspur. The children who grew up and became the state. Chhattisgarh formed November 1, 2000 — a recognition delayed by fifty-three years.

**Key Final Dialogue:**
Amir Hashmi (Preface): *"Johar Gandhi is The Reminiscence Book of Record of the Freedom Fighters of the Diversity Land of Erstwhile Central Province of British India... Chhattisgarh gave strength and unconditional support to Mahatma Gandhi for the successful Indian freedom movement."*

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
The book closes. The series closes. A final image: Chhattisgarh — its forests, its rivers, its people — present day. *"The words recorded on the pages of history may be blurred but the words inscribed in the mind, heart of the people are indelible."*

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
End card. The entire cast of characters — all 20 episodes — flashes in montage. Each name. Each face. Each act of courage. One word over all of them: *JOHAR.*

**Final Slide 37:**
Black screen. White text:
*"JOHAR GANDHI — A Meer Foundation Initiative"*
*"In memory of the freedom fighters of Chhattisgarh whose names history forgot."*

## SECTION 4: NARRATIVE INTERCONNECTIVITY MAP

**Recurring Motifs:**
- **The Third-Class Compartment** — S1E08 (Gandhi in 3rd class at Raipur platform) echoes in S2E01 (Gandhi still traveling simply in 1933)
- **The Tilak Fund / Harijan Fund** — S1E07, S1E08, S1E09 (building) → S2E04, S2E05, S2E06 (reaping — women donating jewelry)
- **The Wall That Falls** — S2E05 (Moti Bagh back wall broken) as metaphor for all walls falling across the series
- **Abbas Bhai's License** — S1E09 (driving Gandhi to Dhamtari) / S2E05 (mentioned again in second visit context) — the cost of proximity
- **Pt. Sundarlal Sharma** — appears in S1E01, S1E05, S1E06, S1E07, S1E10, S2E03, S2E04 — the spine of both series
- **The Promise to Bilaspur** — made in S1E08, fulfilled in S2E06
- **Jaistambh Chowk** — introduced in S1E04 (Veer Narayan Singh martyred here) → mentioned again in S2E09 (as living landmark of sacrifice)
- **Gundadhur** — S1E03 (Bhumkal 1910) → referenced in S1E05, S2E10 (still waiting)

**Character Arcs Across Series:**
- Pt. Ravi Shankar Shukla: First Secretary of Raipur DCC (S1E08) → First CM of MP (S2E09)
- Dr. Radhabai: Nari Jagran (S1E10) → Lathi charge (S2E03)
- Dayavati: Tamora Satyagraha (S2E02) → Gandhi's reason for visiting Tamora (S2E07)

## SECTION 5: VISUAL LANGUAGE GUIDE

**Series 1 — MOHANDAS (1857–1920)**
- Colour palette: Deep ochres, dark forest greens, colonial sepia
- Style: Ink-wash illustrations, rough textures, dramatic shadows
- Recurring motifs: Forest floors, chains, flames, railway tracks, colonial documents
- Typography: Heavy serif, aged paper feel

**Series 2 — THE MAHATMA (1920–1947)**
- Colour palette: Warm golds, amber, white light, morning hues
- Style: Documentary-realist, softer edges, more photographic in feel
- Recurring motifs: Crowds, garlands, spinning wheels, temple doors opening
- Typography: Cleaner, more confident, movement toward light

**Key Visual Contrasts:**
- Episode 1 (dark forest, ancient forts) vs Episode 10 (modern Chhattisgarh skyline dissolving into the same forest)
- Gaind Singh hanged (S1E02) vs Harijans entering temple (S2E04) — doors close and open
- Train leaving Nagpur (S1E10 end) vs train arriving at Durg (S2E01) — 13 years on the same rails

## SECTION 6: WRITING INSTRUCTIONS FOR AI DRAFTING AGENT

When generating slide text for any episode:

1. **Source fidelity is absolute.** Every word of `text` content must come directly from `book_text.txt`. No invention, no paraphrase.

2. **Slide text length:** 2–4 sentences maximum per slide. Natural reading pause at each slide boundary.

3. **`visualDescription` format:** Write as a cinematic image prompt. Include: setting, characters, lighting, camera angle, emotional tone. Example: *"Medium shot. Babu Chhotelal Srivastava standing in flooded paddy field, British tax notice in his hand, farmers behind him, monsoon sky, defiance in his expression. Ink-wash illustration style, colonial era, ochre and green palette."*

4. **`emotion` field values:** `establishing` | `building` | `tension` | `climax` | `resolution` | `reflection`

5. **`animation` field values:** `zoomIn` | `zoomOut` | `panLeft` | `panRight` | `fadeIn` — alternate per slide, avoid repeating the same animation more than twice consecutively.

6. **Slide 1** is always the hero card. Type: `title`. Contains the episode's defining quote.

7. **Slides 2–7** are the cold open — context-setting, high visual impact, shorter text.

8. **Slides 8–33** are the main narrative arc.

9. **Slides 34–35** are the climax — the emotional peak of the episode.

10. **Slide 36** is the resolution — quieter, reflective.

11. **Slide 37** is the cliffhanger/bridge — one sentence pointing to the next episode.
