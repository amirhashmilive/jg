### S2E01 — THIRTEEN YEARS

**Purpose:** Bridge episode. What happened between 1920 and 1933. Civil Disobedience, Salt March impact in CG, the Dandi connection, Jungle Satyagrahas, the Quit India buildup. Chhattisgarh changed — and Gandhi changed.

**Source Chapters:** Gandhian Independence Movement — Civil Disobedience, Salt March, Jungle Satyagrahas 1930–1932

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: A split image — young Mohandas (1920) on the left, The Mahatma (1933) on the right. Thirteen years of history between them, shown as a river of faces, marches, jail bars, and spinning wheels.
Text: *"Gandhi came to Chhattisgarh twice, the first time as Mohandas, the second time as a Mahatma."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: 1929. Lahore Congress. Jawaharlal Nehru chairs. Resolution: complete independence — Purna Swaraj.
- Slide 3: March 12, 1930. Gandhi and 79 companions walk toward Dandi. 200 miles. 24 days.
- Slide 4: April 6, 1930. After morning prayer, Gandhi makes salt on the seashore. The law is broken.
- Slide 5: The fire spreads to Chhattisgarh. In Dhamtari, Pt. Narayan Rao Medhawale makes salt from soil using an ancient method. Karan C. Tejpal buys two grams of it for Rs 61.
- Slide 6: Gandhi arrested May 5, 1930. Jungle Satyagrahas erupt across Chhattisgarh.
- Slide 7: 1932. Congress declared illegal. Satyagraha continues. Leaders imprisoned in Raipur, Nagpur, Jabalpur, Amravati.

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
The scale of the 1930s movement in Chhattisgarh. The Sihawa Jungle Satyagraha led by Shyamlal Som and Pt. Sundarlal Sharma. Hundreds of tribals arrested. The Tamora Jungle Satyagraha 1930 — a preview of S2E02. The Raipur Congress Bhavan constructed 1940, inaugurated by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel. The Raipur Conspiracy Case — Parasram Soni, BB Sur making explosives. The personal Satyagraha of 1940. The Quit India Movement of 1942 — *"Karo ya Maro."* And through it all: Gandhi's promise to Dr. Ambedkar — to fight untouchability for life.

**Key Moment:**
*"In November 1933, Gandhi was engaged in the works of untouchable emancipation. Gandhi started a nationwide journey to prepare the public against untouchability — it was a coincidence that his journey started from Chhattisgarh."*

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
The Poona Pact. Gandhi's vow to Ambedkar. Sevagram Ashram becomes the command centre of truth and non-violence. The second journey to Chhattisgarh is now decided.

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
November 22, 1933. A train approaches Durg. The Mahatma is coming.

**Cliffhanger (Slide 37):**
*"The fire of Tamora could not be forgotten. Episode 2: The Fire of Tamora."*

### S2E02 — THE FIRE OF TAMORA

**Purpose:** The Tamora Jungle Satyagraha 1930 — the most detailed and dramatic non-violent resistance episode in the book. Dayavati's bravery. Police brutality. 17 days of resistance. Gandhi visited Tamora in 1933 specifically because he heard of this Satyagraha.

**Source Chapter:** Gandhian Independence Movement — Tamora Jungle Satyagraha 1930 (full section)

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: Dayavati — young Gond woman — standing before a British soldier's rifle, chest forward, not moving. Forest behind her. The soldier hesitating.
Text: *"A girl named Dayavati and other women were on their way to the place designated for women when a lathi charge took place."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: Tamora village, Mahasamund tehsil. The British Forest Act has banned people from their own forest.
- Slide 3: A forest watchman catches cattle. Demands written admission that they grazed in the forest. The villagers refuse.
- Slide 4: The forest department files a false lawsuit. The court finds it false. But the Congress Committee sends investigators.
- Slide 5: Shankarrao Ganodwale and Yeti Yatan Lal arrive. The villagers agree to Jungle Satyagraha. 90 are selected.
- Slide 6: September 6, 1930. Resolution passed. September 8: the first batch walks toward the forest.
- Slide 7: No police on the first day. No arrests. The second day, thousands gather. The police arrive.

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
Day by day account of the 17-day Satyagraha — the daily meetings, the batches of Satyagrahis, the arrests, the lathi charges, Section 144, the looting by police. Paklu — the villager who refuses forced labour, is beaten, his head split open, carried away blood-soaked. Itwari Kenwat refusing to carry Kanwar. Women standing firm when men flee. Police eating from villagers' houses, looting crops, chickens, ghee. The 90-year-old Dharamu Kalar, deaf and paralyzed, somehow reaching the meeting place — beaten with sticks by police.

**Key Moment:**
*"The police started trying to remove the women from the bayonets but they were not ready to move from their place. Dayavati told that men started running away due to fear of being beaten with sticks, but women remained standing at the meeting place."*

**Key Moment 2:**
Amar Singh of Khamharmuda, guarding his house from police looting, is beaten and ordered to shout *"Long live the King."* He says: *"Mahatma Gandhi ki Jai."* He is beaten more. His wife steps forward. She is beaten too.

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
Raghuvar Singh Dewan — the Malguzar of Tamora — sentenced to three months and fined Rs 200. His niece Dayavati had slapped a British government official during the lathi charge. The village becomes *Gandhigram Tamora.*

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
*"Gandhi had come to Tamora in 1933 after hearing about this Satyagraha. Since then the identity of the village was associated with Gandhi."*

**Cliffhanger (Slide 37):**
*"They were the women who would not move. Episode 3: Women of Iron."*

### S2E03 — WOMEN OF IRON

**Purpose:** The women's freedom movement chapter. Dr. Radhabai, Rohini Bai Parganiha, Phoolkunwar Bai, Kekti Bai Baghel, Dayavati, Daya Bai, Manotara Bai, Bhagwati Bai. The charkha as economic weapon. Gandhi's understanding of women's power.

**Source Chapter:** Gandhian Movement and Women of Chhattisgarh

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: A circle of women, different ages and castes, each at a charkha, spinning. The thread from each wheel weaves into one fabric — the Indian flag.
Text: *"Mahatma Gandhi considered women superior to men for non-violent movements. He called upon women to come forward to fulfill their responsibility for the country."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: The charkha. *"Women adopted Bapu's Charkha, and brought the biggest economy of the British, the textile mill, on the verge of closure."*
- Slide 3: Textile mills — the largest organised industry of the British. Gandhi's boycott targeted cloth. Men could not easily boycott clothing. Women could — by making it themselves.
- Slide 4: Three decades of charkha: *"wreaked havoc on the British economy across the country."*
- Slide 5: Dr. Radhabai — who took the Swadeshi movement door to door in Chhattisgarh.
- Slide 6: Babu Chhotelal Srivastava's house in Koshtapara — a haven for women's charkha production.
- Slide 7: The women of the freedom movement named: Rohini Bai Parganiha, Phoolkunwar Bai, Bela Bai, Kekti Bai Baghel, Rukmini Bai, Daya Bai, Manotara Bai, Bhagwati Bai.

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
Gandhi's philosophy on women — *"She believed that freedom fight cannot be conducted successfully till women do not participate actively in public life nor can Swaraj be attained."* The Tamora women standing before bayonets. The women of Tanwat-Nawapara chasing police with sticks after men fled to the jungle. Women at the Bilaspur rally donating a bag of Rs 1000, then donating their jewelry when Gandhi called it little. The women of the Jaitusav Math gathering, December 21, 1920 — Muslim and Hindu women together. Dayawati of Tamora given her own sequence.

**Key Dialogue:**
Women of Saliha village (Tanwat-Nawapara), confronting police and the Zamindar's men: *"Women, apprehensive about the lives of men, left their homes with sticks. She stood before the police with the determination to kill, while seeing the ruthless appearance of the women, the police and the landlord's goons thought it better to run away."*

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
Dr. Radhabai — beaten in a lathi charge at Raipur station, blood on her forehead — *arrested.* She does not stop.

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
The women of Chhattisgarh receive no official recognition in history books. Their names are here.

**Cliffhanger (Slide 37):**
*"On November 22, 1933, a motor car enters Durg. Episode 4: The Mahatma Returns."*

### S2E04 — THE MAHATMA RETURNS

**Purpose:** November 22, 1933 — arrival at Durg. The integrated school. Gandhi eating with Harijan children. The public meeting. The same night, the historic procession to Raipur. 13 years distilled into one day.

**Source Chapter:** 1933: Mahatma Gandhi's Second Visit — November 22 section

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: Gandhi eating a meal seated alongside Harijan children at Baithd School, Shanichari Bazar, Durg. Simple sackcloth on the floor. The principal watching. Children eating without fear.
Text: *"Gandhi also had food while sitting with the children of the untouchable class at Baithd School located at Shanichari Bazar."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: November 22, 1933. Gandhi arrives in Durg with his secretary Mahadev Desai, Thakkar Bapa, Kumari Meera Ben (Madelaine Slade), and Jamunalal Bajaj's daughter Madalsa Narayan.
- Slide 3: Untouchability is the mission. Gandhi's nationwide Harijan tour — and Chhattisgarh is its starting point.
- Slide 4: Durg. Ghanshyam Singh Gupta tells Gandhi about a school: since 1926, children of upper castes and Harijans have studied sitting on the same sackcloth. Established 1925.
- Slide 5: Gandhi visits. He sees it. He is moved.
- Slide 6: Gandhi eats with the Harijan children. Not a symbolic gesture — a meal. Together.
- Slide 7: Evening. Huge public meeting at Moti Bagh pond grounds, Durg. Gandhi speaks.

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
Gandhi learns about Pt. Sundarlal Sharma's work on untouchability — begun in 1917, before Gandhi made it his central cause. His response: *"Harijan Uddhar mein aap mere bhi guru hain"* — You are my guru in this work. The night procession from Amapara to Budhapara, Raipur. Three hours to travel a short distance. Aarti performed at every step. Flower garlands. Lit lamps on both sides of the road. Gandhi reaches the residence of Pt. Ravishankar Shukla at Budhapara. The crowd's estimate tells the story: *it took more than three hours to reach Shukla Niwas from Amapara.*

**Key Dialogue:**
Gandhi to Pt. Sundarlal Sharma: *"Harijan Uddhar mein aap mere bhi guru hain"* — *"You are even my Guru in the salvation of Harijans."*

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
The procession reaches Shukla Niwas. The crowd will not disperse. Gandhi stands at the window and greets them. The city has not seen anything like this.

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
Gandhi will remain in Raipur until November 28. Each day, a different city. Each night, a return. The centre holds.

**Cliffhanger (Slide 37):**
*"Tomorrow: one lakh people. A broken wall. Episode 5: The Procession of Lamps."*

### S2E05 — THE PROCESSION OF LAMPS

**Purpose:** November 23, 1933 — Moti Bagh Khadi & Swadeshi Exhibition. One lakh crowd. Loudspeaker fails. The back wall of the garden broken to let Gandhi exit. The Satnam Ashram visit. The ring auction. Gandhi as *Baniya.*

**Source Chapter:** 1933: Second Visit — November 23 section

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: Victoria Garden (Moti Bagh), Raipur. An aerial view of one lakh people — the crowd so dense that individual faces are indistinguishable, a sea of humanity, Gandhi invisible at the center.
Text: *"There was a crowd of more than one lakh on this occasion. It was as if the Raipur district was overflowing."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: November 23. Four in the evening. Gandhi comes for the Khadi and Swadeshi Exhibition at Victoria Garden.
- Slide 3: Yati Yatan Lal's diary: *"There was a crowd of more than one lakh. Wherever Narmund, only Narmund was seen."*
- Slide 4: Gandhi's car arrives. *"The whole dam of the system broke."* The car is caught in the whirlpool of people.
- Slide 5: The loudspeaker deteriorates. No one can hear Gandhi exactly. The crowd does not leave.
- Slide 6: To exit, *"The back wall of the garden had to be broken."* A wall falls before Gandhi.
- Slide 7: Pt. Ramdayal Tiwari, author of *Gandhi Mimamsa,* watching: *"Welcoming Gandhi is a fierce display of people's reverence."*

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
The Satnam Ashram visit. Gandhi is informed this was established by Pt. Sundarlal Sharma's efforts. His words: *"Pt. Sundarlal Sharma is senior than me in the work of salvation of Harijans. Those who have done an ideal in conducting this important work of the country before me."* Gandhi goes to Kanhaiyalal Verma's house in Nayapara. Women's gathering. He spreads his bag for the Harijan fund. Jetha Bai donates her gold ring. Gandhi: *"I am a Baniya, I will auction it."* At Gandhi Chowk meeting — a ring worth Rs 15 is auctioned for Rs 500. The Rajkumar College speech: *"Children must come in contact with the British and take their virtues but try to avoid their national miseries like alcoholism and gambling."* The princes of Chhattisgarh studying there — Gandhi knows this and addresses it directly.

**Key Dialogue:**
Gandhi (holding Jetha Bai's ring): *"I am a Baniya (tradesman), I will auction it."*
A ring worth Rs 15 — sold for Rs 500 in the name of Harijan uplift.

Gandhi at Rajkumar College: *"Unfortunately we think that the king is a king, he cannot have any relation with the common people. So we see that a separate school has to be opened for you."*

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
The Rajkumar College speech. Gandhi challenges the princes of Chhattisgarh directly. A complaint is filed against Principal Smith Pearce for allowing it. Gandhi speaks truth to hereditary power.

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
November 23 ends. Tomorrow: Bilaspur — thirteen years after Gandhi promised he would come.

**Cliffhanger (Slide 37):**
*"He promised Bilaspur in 1920. He is thirteen years late. They have been waiting. Episode 6: The Sea of Bilaspur."*
