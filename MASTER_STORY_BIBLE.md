## SECTION 1: SERIES OVERVIEW

### Logline
*Two visits. Two Gandhis. One land that history forgot — and refused to be forgotten.*

### Overall Thematic Arc
JOHAR GANDHI chronicles the untold story of Mahatma Gandhi's two visits to Chhattisgarh through the lens of the people who received him — tribal rebels, peasant farmers, women Satyagrahis, reformers, and ordinary citizens who gave everything for a freedom most of them would not live to see. The series moves from the blood of the first martyr (1825) to the hope of independence (1947), using Gandhi's two arrivals as the twin poles of a vast human story.

**Series 1 — MOHANDAS:** Gandhi arrives as a man still becoming himself. The land he enters has been bleeding for a century. The series asks: what does it take to wake a land?

**Series 2 — THE MAHATMA:** Gandhi returns as a legend. The land has already answered the call. The series asks: what does it cost to see freedom — and what remains after?

### Key Characters and Their Arcs

**Mahatma Gandhi**
First seen as Mohandas — a lawyer, an organiser, a man of principle but not yet myth. By Series 2 he is The Mahatma — an icon who must reconcile his legend with the living suffering before him. His arc: *man → symbol → human again.*

**Pt. Sundarlal Sharma**
The bridge between Gandhi and Chhattisgarh. A zamindar who gave up privilege for reform, he fought untouchability before Gandhi did. Gandhi himself said: *"Harijan Uddhar mein aap mere bhi guru hain."* Arc: *pioneer → connector → vindicated elder.*

**Babu Chhotelal Srivastava**
The organiser of the Kandel Canal Satyagraha. Not a poet or philosopher — a man who simply refused to let his farmers be taxed unjustly. Arc: *local leader → national catalyst → unsung hero.*

**Thakur Pyarelal Singh**
Labour leader, lawyer who gave up law, voice of the mill workers of Rajnandgaon. Arc: *advocate → sacrificer → martyr.*

**Dr. Radhabai**
The woman who carried the Swadeshi movement door to door, who bled in lathi charges and did not stop. Arc: *reformer → warrior → symbol.*

**Dayavati of Tamora**
The young Gond woman who stood before a soldier's gun and did not move. Arc: *farmer → flash of lightning → legend.*

**Veer Narayan Singh**
The first martyr of 1857 in Chhattisgarh — who broke a moneylender's godown to feed the starving, was executed at Jaistambh Chowk, and whose blood became the soil. Arc: *zamindar → rebel → eternal.*

**Gundadhur**
The lionheart of Bhumkal 1910 — the earthquake that shook Bastar. Disappeared without trace. Arc: *leader → legend → ghost the land still waits for.*

### S1E01 — THE LAND OF 36 FORTS

**Purpose:** Establish the world. Introduce Chhattisgarh as a land of ancient identity, colonial wound, and living resistance. Lay the philosophical foundation for everything that follows.

**Source Chapters:** Introduction, "Mahatma Gandhi in Chhattisgarh" (intro section), Preface

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: Ancient forts silhouetted at dawn over Sal forest. Title: *MOHANDAS — The Land of 36 Forts*
Text: *"Johar Gandhi is The Reminiscence Book of Record of the Freedom Fighters of the Diversity Land of Erstwhile Central Province of British India."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: Map of undivided Central Provinces. Chhattisgarh glowing within it.
- Slide 3: The 36 ancient forts of Chhattisgarh — from which the land takes its name.
- Slide 4: Dense Sal forests, rivers, tribal villages — the world Gandhi will enter.
- Slide 5: Gandhi — a small silhouette on a vast railway platform.
- Slide 6: *"Gandhi had a very wide influence in the state of Chhattisgarh, he had visited this province only twice, but without being here physically and without any direct communication, his ideology was widely spread to the far-flung areas of this state."*
- Slide 7: Empty courtyards, fading photographs — *"There is no information about many of the agitators who were involved in the Indian freedom movement from Chhattisgarh."*

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
The story of Gandhi's identity — from South Africa to India — contextualised against Chhattisgarh's political awakening. Leaders like Madhavrao Sapre, Vamanrao Lakhe, Pt. Sundarlal Sharma introduced. The death of Lokmanya Tilak in 1920 creates the vacuum Gandhi fills. The Kandel Canal Satyagraha introduced as the immediate trigger for his first visit.

**Key Dialogue:**
*"We are no more Hindu and Muslims, but Hindustanis in the strictest sense."* — Ravi Shankar Shukla, 1920

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
Pt. Sundarlal Sharma boards a train to Calcutta on December 2, 1920. His mission: bring Gandhi to Chhattisgarh.
*"The British accepted the demands of Kandel Satyagrahis even before Mahatma Gandhi reached Raipur."*

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
Gandhi steps onto the Raipur platform. The crowd sees his sandals first. A new chapter begins.

**Cliffhanger (Slide 37):**
Title card: *"The blood that made this soil. Episode 2: Blood of the First Martyr."*

### S1E02 — BLOOD OF THE FIRST MARTYR

**Purpose:** Establish Chhattisgarh's century-long tradition of resistance. Introduce the tribal uprisings as the roots of the freedom movement. Honour Gaind Singh as Shaheed Shiromani.

**Source Chapter:** Early Era of Revolutionaries in Chhattisgarh — Revolt of Paralkot 1825

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: Gaind Singh standing on a hilltop in the forest at dawn, traditional weapons, British soldiers in the valley below.
Text: *"Gaind Singh became immortal as the first martyr in the history of Chhattisgarh."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: 1820s. The Bhonsle dynasty has fallen. British flags rise over Chhattisgarh.
- Slide 3: Paralkot Zamindari — 165 villages, the banks of river Kotri, three rivers meeting.
- Slide 4: Abhujmadi tribal life — Ghotul, bows, drums, forest community.
- Slide 5: British officers arrive — Captain Pebe establishes stronghold at Paralkot.
- Slide 6: Forced taxes, harvesting of forest, interference in tribal home.
- Slide 7: 1824. Gaind Singh rises. *"Gaind Singh, leading all the Abhujmadis, called for British-free Bastar."*

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
The Revolt of Paralkot in full detail. Guerrilla warfare — ambushes from forest, drums summoning warriors, groups of five to five hundred. Women participating equally. The Ghotul as strategy room. The British-Maratha combined force besieging Paralkot on January 10, 1825. Traditional arrows versus modern guns. The siege. The arrest.

**Key Dialogue (reconstructed from book):**
*"Ek saal vriksh ke pichhe, ek vyakti ka sir"* — the Sal tree rebellion slogan (from Koei Revolt, contextualised here as the spirit of all tribal resistance)

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
January 20, 1825. Gaind Singh is hanged in front of the palace of Paralkot.
*"The soil of Dakshin Kosal lit up with the blood of its first martyr son, and this light further set fire to the piles of dry leaves of the rebellions."*

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
The Paralkot Zamindar family today — living in oblivion. The area now contains the city of Pakhanjur. The sacrifice forgotten.

**Cliffhanger (Slide 37):**
*"But the fire never died. Episode 3: The Earthquake of Bastar."*

### S1E03 — THE EARTHQUAKE OF BASTAR

**Purpose:** Complete the arc of tribal revolts. Build to Gundadhur and Bhumkal 1910 as the final, greatest eruption before the Gandhian era. Establish the pattern: tribals resist → British crush → the land remembers.

**Source Chapter:** Tribal Revolts — Revolt of Halba through Revolt of Bhumkal 1910

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: Gundadhur leading thousands of tribals through Bastar forest, flags of sal leaves, dawn light through the trees.
Text: *"Bhumak or Bhumkal means earthquake or vibration of the land."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: Timeline of revolts — Halba 1774, Bhopalpatnam 1795, Paralkot 1825... a chain of fire.
- Slide 3: The Revolt of Tarapur 1842 — Lal Dalganjan Singh vs illegal taxation.
- Slide 4: The Revolt of Meria 1842–63 — Hidma Manjhi defending the Dantewada temple tradition.
- Slide 5: The Revolt of Koei 1859 — *"Ek saal vriksh ke pichhe, ek vyakti ka sir"* — defending the Sal tree.
- Slide 6: The Revolt of Muria 1876 — Jhada Sirha surrounding the king's palace, demanding the Diwan's removal. Success.
- Slide 7: 1910. Bastar. Something bigger is coming.

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
Bhumkal 1910 in full. Gundadhur's leadership. The united tribal front — Kunwar Bahadur Singh, Bala Prasad, Dular Singh, Nazir. Traditional weapons versus British guns. The martyrs: Lal Lokendra Singh in Elichpur jail, Rani Sumran Kunwar in Raipur jail. 78 convicted, 26 died in Raipur Jail. Names recorded: Asu, Kidariya, Korli, Kola, Chandu...

**Key Moment:**
*"The people of Bastar are still waiting for Gundadhur."* — He disappeared. No confirmed death. The land still listens.

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
The names of the dead read out like a roll call. The known martyrs of Bhumkal. A reckoning with forgotten sacrifice.

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
Bhumkal Day — February 10 — still celebrated every year in Bastar. The living memory.

**Cliffhanger (Slide 37):**
*"From tribal rebellion to national uprising. Episode 4: The Cannon at Jaistambh."*

### S1E04 — THE CANNON AT JAISTAMBH

**Purpose:** The Indian Rebellion of 1857 in Chhattisgarh. Veer Narayan Singh as the defining martyr of this era. The 18 soldiers of the Raipur mutiny. Establish the tradition of organised armed resistance.

**Source Chapter:** The Indian Rebellion of 1857 in Chhattisgarh

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: Veer Narayan Singh standing at the intersection of Raipur — Jaistambh Chowk — British cannons aimed at him, head raised.
Text: *"Veer Narayan Singh challenged the British policies even before the Revolt of 1857."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: May 10, 1857. Meerut. The spark ignites. News reaches Chhattisgarh.
- Slide 3: Bahadur Shah Zafar proclaimed emperor. *"Bahadur Shah Zafar Zindabad"* echoes across India.
- Slide 4: Sonakhan, 1856. Famine. A moneylender refuses food to the starving.
- Slide 5: Veer Narayan Singh breaks the godown. Distributes grain to the people.
- Slide 6: He is arrested on false charges. But in jail, the rebellion finds him.
- Slide 7: He escapes. Forms an army of 500 men. The British march against him.

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
The capture, trial, and execution of Veer Narayan Singh on December 10, 1857 at Jaistambh Chowk. The British force all soldiers to watch — meant to terrorise. The Raipur Military Mutiny of January 18, 1858 — 39 days later. Hanuman Singh kills Sergeant Major Sidbell. The 17 martyrs of the 3rd Regular Regiment. Their names: Ghazi Khan, Havaldar Badlu, Hayat Golanadaj, Munim, Shivnarayan, Pannalal, Maatadin, Bukaram, Bilhu, Akbar Hussain, Lalsingh, Bablu, Paranand, Shobharam, Durgaprasad, Nazar Muhammad, Shiva, Devidin.

**Key Moment:**
*"People of all castes and religions were among the seventeen martyrs — a symbol of the great sense of national interest instilled in the people of this region."*

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
Hanuman Singh escapes. Rs 500 reward. Never caught. Never found. *"After this incident no details of him were received, he never came back."*

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
The Revolt of Sambalpur — Surendra Sai's decades of resistance, dying blind in Asirgarh Fort 1884. *"The Last Martyr of the Revolt of 1857."*

**Cliffhanger (Slide 37):**
*"The fire of armed revolt dims. A new kind of warrior emerges. Episode 5: Awakening."*

### S1E05 — AWAKENING

**Purpose:** The bridge between armed revolt and Gandhian non-cooperation. The intellectual and social awakening of Chhattisgarh — Congress established, Arya Samaj reforms, Swadeshi movement, Goraksha campaign, the pioneers of the region's modern identity.

**Source Chapters:** Gandhian Independence Movement in Chhattisgarh — opening sections; Indian National Congress in Chhattisgarh

**Slide 1 — Hero Card:**
Visual: Madhavrao Sapre at a printing press, first issue of *Chhattisgarh Mitra* 1900, lamplight, determination.
Text: *"Pt. Madhavrao Sapre was the first to inspire the development and national awakening of Chhattisgarh."*

**Cold Open (Slides 2–7):**
- Slide 2: 1889 — Bombay. The annual INC session. 214 delegates from CP & Berar attend. Names: CM Thakkar, Pt. Ramdayal Tiwari, Madhavrao Sapre, Vamanrao Lakhe.
- Slide 3: 1900. *Chhattisgarh Mitra* is published. The first monthly magazine in the region.
- Slide 4: The Arya Samaj establishes in Raipur. Social reform: against untouchability, for widow marriage, women's education.
- Slide 5: 1905. The Swadeshi Movement. Partition of Bengal. The boycott reaches Chhattisgarh.
- Slide 6: Goraksha Song — *"Gaiyya roi, roi karat pukaar, More praan bacha lo hari..."* — written by Wali Mohammad, Hindu-Muslim unity in one poem.
- Slide 7: 1906. CM Thakkar founds Congress in Raipur. Pt. Sundarlal Sharma joins.

**Narrative Arc (Slides 8–33):**
The Yagyopavit movement — Sundarlal Sharma makes all castes equal before the sacred thread. The 1907 Surat split — Chhattisgarh produces both Garam Dal and Naram Dal leaders. Home Rule League 1917 — Ravi Shankar Shukla, Raghavendra Rao, Pyarelal Singh, Ghanshyam Singh Gupta all building branches. The 1918 Sixth Provincial Conference in Raipur. Shukla's first arrest and release — his rise as the dominant leader.

**Key Dialogue:**
*"We are no more Hindu and Muslims, but Hindustanis in the strictest sense."* — Ravi Shankar Shukla at the 1920 Khilafat sub-committee meeting.

**Climax (Slides 34–35):**
1918. Pt. Sundarlal Sharma's 21-day fast against untouchability. Chhabilal Choubey fasting alongside. The Janeu movement — all castes wearing the sacred thread.

**Resolution (Slide 36):**
The first slaughterhouses in India closed through mass movement — village Karmandih and Ghobani, 1914 — entirely through non-violent people's pressure. A sign of what is coming.

**Cliffhanger (Slide 37):**
*"In a small village called Kandel, the farmers are waiting. Episode 6: The Water and the Tax."*
